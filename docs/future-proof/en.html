Why now

If your company runs stack ranking tomorrow — who gets cut first?

Not the junior: they're cheap and, with AI, close tasks faster than a year ago. Not the senior: they're the one who says "we're solving the wrong problem" — and that's worth more right now than the code that gets written.

The middle has grown enough to cost a lot — but not enough to justify it. They write good code. They know the codebase. They're reliable. But all of that is execution. And execution is getting cheaper faster than you can reach your next performance review.

You're vulnerable not because you work badly. But because you work well in exactly the layer that's losing its value. This isn't a sentence — it's a diagnosis. And a diagnosis can be treated.

  • —My knowledge seems fine. I get the work done, I can do whatever's needed.
  • —I figure out new things on my own. I've mastered the patterns and reproduce them efficiently.
  • —People value me. But the feeling of moving forward — not so much.
  • —The seniors around me are different somehow, but that "somehow" is hard to name.
  • —A suspicion: it's not about how much I know. But what it is about — unclear.
What changed

The stronger one today isn't whoever writes code faster.

It's whoever better understands: what exactly to do, why this way, under which constraints, with which consequences — and how to explain it to people, the team, and AI. Code became cheap raw material. Judgment is what's getting more valuable. And here's the fork: either you're an engineer who sets the tasks and owns the decisions — or you're a code-generation operator. The operator doesn't survive stack ranking.

The mechanism

A recipe you can't drop a single word from.

For you to be the one chosen when it's time to decide "who to promote and who to cut," you need one thing — and it's been proven on real careers, not in theory:

regularly deliver a lot of visible results
Regularly → architecture

With AI, your productivity peaks at the start, then everything bogs down: the agent mimics the project's chaos and multiplies it. Only architecture lets you hold the pace for months. Good architecture is the kind that lets you defer irreversible decisions for as long as possible.

A lot → AI-agents

The "a lot of results via overtime" path leads to burnout. The only alternative is agents. But they deliver "a lot" only for someone who can frame a task so the agent solves it instead of guessing.

Visible → communication

You can lay down a brilliant platform and still get cut — because your manager didn't understand your value. Visibility isn't self-promotion. It's a skill, and it's available to introverts: what matters is the efficiency of your communication, not the volume.

Results → solving problems

A result isn't code. A result is a solved business problem. Clarify the requirement, pick a solution that fits the constraints, see it through, and show the link to the goal. That's exactly what gets evaluated when they decide who to keep.

Try it on yourself

A fragment of one topic from the base. Right now, in three minutes.

The topic is called "The stakeholders' language: translating from technical" — it's already inside. Here's a typical conversation about tech debt, and the same conversation after one shift.

As usual

you:We need to refactor this module.

PM:Why?

you:The code is bad.

PM:But the features work, right?

you:Well, yeah, but…

PM:Then we'll wait.

After the shift

you:We need to refactor this module.

PM:Why?

you:Every new feature here takes 3 times longer. The last one — 4 days instead of a day and a half. The next one from the backlog will take a week instead of two days if we don't fix it.

This isn't an evil PM. It's a person who wasn't handed the information in the right format. You talked about code — but a manager knows how to count time. The conversation was lost before it began.

Action for today

Take one piece of tech debt that annoys you. Frame a single sentence: "Because of [technical problem], every time we [action], we lose [time]." That's how every topic in the guild works: a compressed concept → a task in your project → the difference is visible within the same week.
