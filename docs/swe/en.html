“I'm stuck. And I don't understand why”

Sound familiar?

  • My knowledge seems fine. I get the work done, I can handle whatever's required.
  • I can figure out a new topic on my own.
  • I'm valued, but there's no sense of movement.
  • The seniors around me are different somehow, but that “something” is hard to name.
  • I study on my own — articles, courses, videos — and I suspect I'm learning the wrong things.
  • I stew in my own thoughts: no one reviews my code, there's nothing to compare it to.
  • And on top of it all — the AI chaos dropped on our heads: it's unclear what to learn and what's actually serious.

If you recognized yourself in at least three of these — that's not impostor syndrome talking. It's the absence of an environment and feedback. On your own, this shift is almost impossible to find.

It's not about you. What people pay for is changing

A junior with AI covers tasks from below. A senior covers them from above — by saying “we're solving the wrong problem.” The middle developer ends up in between: writes good, reliable code, knows the codebase. But all of that is execution. And execution is getting cheaper faster than you can reach your next performance review.

“Middle developers are vulnerable not because they work badly. They're vulnerable because they work well in exactly the layer that's losing value. It's not a sentence. It's a diagnosis”— Illya Klymov

And when stack ranking kicks in at your company — “you'll be counted, whether you want it or not.” The one who stands out won't be the one who writes code faster. Code no longer sets anyone apart.

“Code, knowledge and access to tools have all gotten cheaper. The only things gaining value are clarity of opinion and the quality of decisions”— Timur Shemsedinov
