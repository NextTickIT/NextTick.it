Why we created the Guild

Courses stopped keeping up with the profession.

Every year an engineer is asked for more: architecture, agents, communication — and all of it at once. A one-off course simply doesn't work that way. So we built a place where an engineer can move up the career ladder calmly: your own track, a knowledge base for when it's “on fire right now,” workshops with senior engineers, and direct access to us. No question is left without an answer.

Why now

Code is getting cheaper. Judgment is getting more expensive.

AI writes a component, a test, and a draft feature faster than you. That doesn't mean you've gotten worse — what people pay for is changing. They used to pay for execution. Now they pay for the decisions AI won't make for you.

It's not about you. The profession is changing — and you can catch up.

Environment

The one thing AI won't give you is engineers nearby: your level and stronger.

A model will answer your question. But it won't argue with your architecture, won't say “I've screwed that up too,” and won't let you lean on someone stronger. That takes an environment: a peer group that grows with you, and senior engineers you can come to with any question.

Show a solution and get an honest breakdown, not a polite “yeah, fine.”
Ask a “dumb” question in a workshop where people answer instead of judging.
Hear how engineers stronger than you reason — every week.

Three tracks

Three directions that help you avoid getting stuck in the role of an executor.

01 / architecture

Architecture

Not patterns for the sake of patterns, but constraints, NFR, ADR, decomposition, the cost of a mistake, and the logic of decisions. So you see the system before it starts to break.

02 / agents

AI agents

Not “write a better prompt,” but a working process: what to hand to an agent, how to set the context, how to verify the result, where to stop, and what to leave to a human.

03 / communication

Communication

Not abstract soft skills, but engineering influence: feedback, refactoring, stakeholders, RFC, and the ability to be heard without formal authority.

Objections

The most common doubts — no pressure and no slick workarounds.

What if I can't keep up?

You don't need to catch up on the archive. Every month is a new topic; you join from the current moment. The base isn't going anywhere.

What if I already know what to do?

Knowing isn't the same as doing. The Guild is an environment and an external push so that knowledge turns into action.

$50 a month — that's 450 over nine months

It's a subscription, not a course: you pay for a month and leave in one click. Compare it to an hour of your time: if the Guild saves you two hours a month on the question of “what to learn,” it has already paid off.

I don't like calls

The core value is the videos, the chat, and the digests. There are two calls a month, and each one has a recording. Live participation is a bonus, not an obligation.

What if soft skills aren't my thing?

You already communicate every day: estimates, risks, PRs, planning. Communication makes your technical thinking heard.

What if AI teaches me everything anyway?

AI answers questions. But it doesn't decide what you should learn, doesn't give you an environment, and doesn't replace engineering judgment.
